About

I am a PhD student in Computer Science at BITS Pilani, advised in work with Poonam Goyal and Navneet Goyal. My research sits at the intersection of information retrieval, retrieval-augmented generation, and applied ML — with a particular interest in systems that have to be both accurate and kind to the people who use them.

Before I came back to campus, I spent a few years as a software engineer in Bengaluru. I built payment and gaming backends at ShareChat, owned credit-card decisioning services at PowerUp Money (formerly UniCards), and this summer I interned as an Applied Scientist at Microsoft, working on Power Pages adoption and maker experience.

The through-line is the same: take a messy real-world problem, find the structure in it, and ship something that holds up in production. I still teach NLP, generative AI, and advanced algorithms, and I still contest-program when I can.

Dual degree first: B.E. Mechanical Engineering and M.Sc. Mathematics at BITS, then a turn into industry, then a PhD in CS. The math never really left.

02

Research

With the Ministry of Tribal Affairs, I turn welfare-scheme documents into posters and animations that low-resource language communities can actually use — culturally faithful, not just translated.

Retrieval · graphs

CTU-FlowRAG

Coherent Thematic Unit segmentation, then role-conditioned retrieval over typed sentence graphs. Built so a welfare document can be queried by the role the reader actually cares about.

  • 0.82 nDCG@10
  • 0.75 MRR@10
  • 0.96 role coverage

3,048 scheme documents · 187,220 sentence units

Code on GitHub

Culture · RAG fusion

QCCAF

A query-conditioned fusion layer that adds local cultural context before image prompts are written. Zero-shot transfer across related low-resource languages, not just the one we trained on.

  • 0.93 nDCG@10 on Santali
  • zero-shot Khasi, Bodo, Garo

New Santali IR benchmark

Latency · bandits

Bandit-Quota

A latency-budgeted contextual bandit for per-query retriever selection. Match an ensemble when you can afford it; skip the rest when you cannot.

  • 2.4–9.3× faster than full ensemble
  • 6 / 7 BEIR datasets match or beat quality

Contextual Thompson sampling
